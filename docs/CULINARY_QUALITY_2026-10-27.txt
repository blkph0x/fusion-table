FUSION TABLE — CULINARY QUALITY IMPROVEMENTS
27 October 2026 | Website recipe engine

Purpose

The user's Aussie–Thai parma and velveted steak with Mornay wrap examples set a
quality benchmark: each ingredient needs a clear job, the cooking order should
develop flavour, crisp surfaces should stay crisp, and the final assembly should
make sense. The changes improve shared cooking instructions across existing meal
and classic formats. They do not establish that these example dishes, or every
generated combination, have been cooked and tasted.

Regional ingredients now have cooking phases

The shared helper in lib/regional-cooking.ts allocates supplied ingredients to
specific preparation, heating, tempering, softening, blooming, liquid, simmering,
removal and finishing stages. The original profile slot is not enough to decide
how an ingredient should be cooked. Whole seeds, ground spices, fresh aromatics,
wet condiments and delicate pastes receive different handling.

Whole seeds can be briefly tempered in allocated oil. Aromatics soften before
ground spices are gently bloomed. Suitable curry and chilli pastes are fried
before the bulk sauce liquid is added. Sweet and salty wet condiments dissolve
after liquid is present. Heat and fragrance cues accompany the timing estimates
so the cook can avoid scorching. If no frying oil was allocated, the helper uses
an explicit wet method rather than silently inventing more oil.

Special cases are handled deliberately. Whole dried ancho chillies are softened
before blending and cooking; their soaking water is separate from sauce liquid.
Annatto seeds infuse the existing oil or liquid and are strained out. Saffron
borrows a portion of the listed liquid. Woody leaves, pods or whole aromatic
pieces have removal instructions where appropriate. Measured portions are
accounted for once; a later reference or removal is not another ingredient use.

Delicate flavours finish close to service

Miso and other delicate pastes are loosened with cooked sauce and added gently
toward the finish. Fresh herbs, citrus, finishing spices and toasted sesame oil
have late actions instead of being put through the entire simmer. This is a
practical default for these sauce templates, not a claim that miso can never be
grilled or that every cuisine uses a spice at the same stage.

Meal instructions now distinguish cooking the sauce base from its final
seasoning. The delicate finish happens when the main has reached its stated
endpoint and the side is ready. Crisp schnitzel and patties keep their sauce
alongside until eating. Stir-fried batches are cooked first, then folded into
the finished sauce without a further prolonged cook. Long smokes schedule the
sauce near service rather than holding a freshly finished sauce for hours.

Classic templates use the same ingredient-aware approach. Wet regional sauces
are kept out of patties and away from direct grill or roasting heat. Pasta is
started for its packet-timed final window and tossed immediately. Cold platters
cool the cooked sauce promptly, refrigerate it, and add their fresh finish
without reheating the chilled ready-to-eat main.

Assembly and regional identity remain coherent

Seeded assembly preserves the selected main, cooking method and accompaniment.
Finishing vegetables are prepared before delicate sauce seasoning, and garnishes
are reserved for final assembly. Crisp dishes are not casually converted into
wet bowls. A broth assembly uses suitable retained cooking liquid, not raw meat
juices or poaching liquid that the method instructed the cook to discard.

The original classic formats retain their authored starch. Regionalising pasta,
burgers, tacos or a potato-topped pie does not add a second full rice, grain or
bread portion. Authored dishes that intentionally combine rice and beans retain
those components. Regional exploration uses the selected profile and explicit
extras; merely sharing a rice or other staple does not import another region's
entire flavour pantry. For example, ordinary Japanese exploration no longer
inherits the Japanese-curry profile's turmeric and curry powder.

Measured, refrigerated stir-fry coating

The helper in lib/main-techniques.ts adds a light maize-starch coating only to
the specified Chinese-family thin, boneless beef, chicken, pork and turkey
stir-fries. Whole, bone-in, minced, offal and sausage forms are excluded; uncertain
slow-cut forms are skipped unless explicitly boneless. Seafood, duck, other
regions and other cooking methods do not receive this coating automatically.

The coating uses approximately 1.5% of the raw meat weight as pure maize starch
and 60 ml cold water per kilogram of meat. One quarter of the main's existing
oil is reserved for the coating; the remaining three quarters stay with the pan.
No extra oil is silently added. For a four-serving 600 g main with 2 Australian
tablespoons of main oil, that is 9 g starch, 36 ml water, 10 ml coating oil and
30 ml pan oil. An Australian tablespoon is 20 ml; the coating instruction gives
its oil portions in millilitres.

The covered meat rests in the refrigerator at 5°C or colder for 15 minutes and
stays chilled until cooking. The instructions include thin cross-grain slicing,
uncrowded batches, disposal of leftover raw coating mixture and cleaning hands,
the bowl and utensils. The recipe's thermometer endpoint still applies. The
coating introduces no bicarbonate, egg or soy-based marinade and does not copy
raw-meat rinsing or a room-temperature rest from the benchmark. Corn or maize
exclusions skip the coating. Product labels still need checking.

Scaling, sequencing and time estimates

Meal draft quantities use a four-serving basis and are scaled once. The coating
ingredient and divided oil allocations retain that basis; its instruction is
scaled to the requested servings. The regional helper also supports quantities
already scaled by the classic engine, avoiding a second scaling pass. Separate
base, sauce and finishing portions are drawn from the same listed totals.

Preparation and cooking estimates now account for the refrigerated coating
rest, regional preparation such as dried-chilli soaking, sauce stages, relevant
braise browning, smoke resting and final assembly. Sides begin near the end of
long cooking where appropriate and follow their own serving instructions;
cold sides are not told to be held hot. These remain estimates: thickness,
equipment, batch count and the main's thermometer endpoint take precedence.

Automated evidence and its limits

The full generation and dietary matrix subtest in tests/meals.test.ts passed
668,430 cases. It exercises five dietary settings, all 90 sides and all supported
main/method pairs for each diet across seven sampled regions: Japanese,
Indonesian, Indian vindaloo, Xinjiang, West African, home kitchen and Afghan.
It is not an exhaustive test of all 76 regions, all random seeds or every cut.
The complete captured run is in:
docs/audit-evidence-2026-10-27/culinary-full-meal-matrix.log

Focused regressions cover ingredient-stage allocation and ordering, delicate
finishing, scaling and oil portions, refrigerated coating, exclusion handling,
classic starch preservation, pasta timing, cold-sauce handling, smoke scheduling
and reproducible recipe sharing. The new coating helper's eight focused tests
passed. The final broader run passed all 380 tests in 42 suites, excluding the
separately completed expensive meals.test.ts matrix suite. That suite passed
all 12 of its tests in a follow-up run. A further 6,840 regional ingredient plans
and 4,250 classic/region/diet cases are exercised inside focused checks.
TypeScript, touched recipe-file lint and the local Node production build passed.
Five existing image-element lint warnings remain in app/page.tsx; no lint errors.
Browser checks verified Thai schnitzel, Chinese beef stir-fry, Japanese pasta and
a chilled Japanese smoked-salmon platter. Evidence lives alongside the matrix log.

This release updates the website. The separately built Android APK is unchanged.

Automated checks establish structural and safety-instruction properties. They
do not measure taste, tenderness, crispness, seasoning balance or real kitchen
timing. Recipes remain template-generated and not kitchen-tested. The benchmark
images are inspiration, not evidence that a generated meal was cooked or that
its dietary suitability is guaranteed.
