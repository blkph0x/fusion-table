What is now working
Recipe and shared-recipe pages automatically check their shopping costs. The
shopping workspace also starts automatically when its area or list changes.
Prices update for changed servings, pantry quantities, ingredients and shopping
area. Reviewed product choices and exclusions survive navigation and late
responses. Available prices appear progressively with their sources, dates and
priced-item coverage. Missing prices stay unknown; a partial subtotal is never
presented as a complete basket. Costs describe packs/weighed amounts to buy,
including leftovers, rather than only the food consumed per serving.

Pictures now have a durable background queue and cache. Missing pictures return
a labelled waiting image immediately, then appear automatically when ready.
Requested pictures take priority over warming; one renderer, bounded queues,
retries, hourly limits and disk limits keep this manageable. Daily warming fills
popular authored choices rather than scheduling twenty million images. Nine
pictures were already cached by the final check. Images keep the complete dish
visible, and shared-recipe titles have a clear reading hierarchy.

The homepage now reports 20,414,810 authored choice combinations, 76 meal regions,
90 sides and 34 classic dishes, and explains what the count includes.

Research applied
Reviewed the actual current local P vs NP research checkpoint v17. Applied sound
conflict learning, backjumping and independent result checking. The solver now
returns SAT, independently certified UNSAT, or UNKNOWN when limits prevent a valid
conclusion. An unfinished search cannot masquerade as no solution. Finite model
enumeration blocks previously emitted assignments; optimal is reserved for verified
exhaustion. Interactive meals compare up to eight compatible checked finishes by
explicit ingredient simplicity, produce, pantry and extra-pan preferences. This is
a bounded best-known objective, not a global taste optimum or a proof of P = NP.

Feedback that can improve meals
Cooked reports and first impressions are separate. Low web scores of 0-2, negative
answers and will-not-make-again require a reason saved atomically with the vote.
Thirteen categories include salt, heat, texture, quantities, timing, instructions,
combinations, availability, cost and cooking/allergen concerns. Optional ingredient
and step references must exist in the exact supplied recipe version.
Actual ingredient amounts, methods and servings bind the report. Legacy and other-
version evidence cannot silently rank a current meal. At least three distinct
anonymous browsers must report cooking an exact version before shared rankings
change, and the adjustment is bounded within eligible comparable candidates.
Browsers are not authenticated people. Serious concerns enter a review section
immediately and prevent positive promotion; they never automatically rewrite
ingredients, cooking endpoints or hard filters. Personal cookbook ratings remain
private and explicitly labelled. Opening a report's current base template may
produce a different version; those reports are not transferred to it.

Pricing safeguards and limits
Narrow plain-product rules can resolve common brand ties, while different food
forms, unsuitable automatic substitutes and obvious non-food products are excluded.
Loose-product weight increments without a verified selling-price basis are not
used as unit prices. Australian tablespoons/teaspoons convert to 20/5 ml only for
volume packs. No food density, count-to-weight conversion or dry/cooked yield is
guessed. Retailer blocking and missing/ambiguous products still require review.
Online chain guides are not verified local branch stock or shelf prices. A live
verification found suitable mushroom and cooking-cream packs and priced both lines;
other real recipe lists correctly remained partial when usable quantity prices
were absent. No prices were invented to fill those gaps.

Measured image and performance results
The isolated production-host check returned a waiting picture in 95 ms, then a real
384-pixel image became ready in the background after about 39 seconds. The public
browser verified the automatic image swap and persistence across a website restart.
The existing local SD-Turbo backend is supervised as fusion-image-model.service,
listening only on 127.0.0.1:7860 and enabled at boot. Website warming is enabled.
No paid cloud image API or key was added. Generated pictures are visualisations of
ideas; they are not photographs of actual cooked meals or visual proof of ingredients.
At this measured serial rate, pre-rendering 20.4 million pictures would take roughly
25 years and several terabytes. Bounded warming plus demand caching is the practical
approach; faster hardware could change those figures.
Local CPU generation while six audit workers ran: seeded meal median 6.3 ms,
p95 10.2 ms; seven-day plan median 725.76 ms, maximum 886.74 ms. These are generation
timings, not complete browser/network latency.
